LightRoom Gallery

A room for photography.

A Philadelphia space shaped by artists, shared looking, and the durable pleasure of a photograph in person.

The story

A gallery with a long local focus.

The Light Room, founded in 2000, has been a gathering place for Philadelphia’s photography community. Its programs have included solo presentations, collaborative projects, salons, and annual exhibitions by members of the LightRoom photography cooperative.

By 2011, the gallery and its related darkroom were active in the Fairmount area. The gallery space at 2024 Wallace Street opened its doors for Philadelphia Open Studio Tours, creating a place where photographers could show work, meet peers, and bring the medium into public view.

Today, LightRoom continues that artist-led tradition. Its exhibitions make room for photographs to be encountered slowly, in conversation with the people who make, collect, and care for them.

The organization

A cooperative for photographers.

The Light Room was founded in 2000 by Albie Wachlin Jr. and Joshua Marowitz. Its newsletter carried the name Philadelphia’s Photographic Arts Co-Op, and the organization described itself as an artist-run non-profit photographic arts center: a community and gallery devoted to fine-art photography for young people and adults alike. The gallery program appears on this site as LightRoom Gallery.

Members were the point of the place. The co-op stated its purpose directly: “Our goal is to foster an environment in which members can achieve their photographic objectives through the use of modern, professional darkroom and studio facilities, ongoing education, and the sharing of ideas.” The Light Room did not offer public classes. Callers looking for open instruction were referred to Project Basho or the Philadelphia Photo Arts Center.

The gallery is at 2024 Wallace Street, and the darkroom was at 1909 Wilcox Street in Fairmount. The working rooms included that darkroom, a small digital lab, and the gallery itself. In 2012, Artblog called The Light Room the oldest and most under-the-radar of Philadelphia’s three community photo art centers, alongside Project Basho and the Philadelphia Photo Arts Center.

Members showed together in annual group exhibitions, often at 3rd Street Gallery in Old City. Wallace Street kept its own calendar of solo presentations, collaborations, and member catalog shows. The gallery still hosts exhibitions, including the shows on view today.
